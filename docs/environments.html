More than
a convincing scene.

A beautiful world can invite an experience. A research environment helps us ask what follows from it.

We study the relationships among sensory context, personal history, and subsequent behavior. The aim is to understand which observations become useful, which distinctions a system must preserve, and when it should ask rather than assume.

Where Prizm

Prizm is part of the work, not the boundary of the mission.

The name appears across person-facing concepts and environment studies. We keep those tracks distinct while exploring the questions they share. A common name does not imply one integrated system or a shared model.

Research resources

Useful worlds.
Meaningful evidence.

Our longer-term ambition is to offer environments, evaluation resources, and permissioned multimodal datasets to other teams studying intelligence.

The opportunity is to make difficult questions testable: what physical experience adds beyond conversation, whether a system handles personal context faithfully, and what transfers into a new setting. External adoption and general transfer remain to be established.

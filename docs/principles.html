RESEARCH PILLARS

Three research pillars

Our work begins with three ideas about experience and intelligence. They explain why we study scent-memory, emotion and affect, and what we hope that research can contribute.

Experience has personal meaning.

A description of a scent cannot tell us what it means to the person encountering it. Autobiographical memory, emotion and present context are part of the experience. We begin here because understanding a person requires more than recognizing the things around them.

Biology informs the inquiry.

Neuroscience, cognitive science and olfactory research give us foundations for asking how experience becomes meaningful and shapes learning. We use AI as a scientific instrument to investigate these questions. A biological analogy can suggest a direction; evidence must establish whether it is useful.

Intelligence needs human context.

A world model may describe what happens without understanding what it means to a particular person. We want to investigate whether learning from human experience can help intelligent systems understand which consequences matter to the people they serve.

Human findings, useful computational methods and improvements in alignment are separate results to establish. None follows automatically from the others.

Intelligence that stays
faithful to people.

Our alignment research asks how understanding human experience can improve the way agents interpret, decide, and act.

Begin with
the person.

Recognizing a preference is not the same as understanding an intention. Remembering a sentence is not the same as using it well.

Human meaning depends on context. It can change, remain uncertain, or be misunderstood. Scentient investigates how sensory experience and personal history might help intelligent systems navigate that complexity.

Questions we can
put to the test.

01

Meaning & intention.

Can richer sensory context help an agent distinguish what a person means from what the system assumes?

02

Correction & continuity.

Can an agent respond to a changed understanding while preserving relevant context that has not changed, including across model transitions?

03

Uncertainty & agency.

Can a system recognize when it should ask, abstain, or leave a decision with the person, while remaining useful?

04

Action & consequence.

Can experience improve subsequent decisions, and which principles carry into new agents, environments, or embodied tasks?

A concrete part of a larger problem.

Our immediate focus is personal alignment: useful conduct that remains faithful to a person’s expressed intentions, context, and authority over their experience. We also study continuity across changing models and agents.

This is a research contribution toward broader alignment questions, not a claim to have solved general AI alignment or established system-wide safety.

Predicting someone’s response does not by itself establish alignment with their intentions. The person’s authority, the system’s uncertainty, and its subsequent conduct remain central.

Three kinds of evidence.

Human encounters tell us about reported experience and usefulness. Synthetic agent tasks let us study defined behavior under controlled conditions. Embodied experiments test perception and action in an environment. Each has its own question; success in one does not establish success in the others.

Ambition with an observable test.

We compare ideas against capable alternatives and ask whether added sensory context or a proposed learning approach produces a useful difference. The aim is to discover which principles deserve to carry forward.
